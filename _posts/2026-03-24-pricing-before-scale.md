A company selling a $29 product to 40,000 customers hit $13 million in annual revenue and still couldn't afford a second engineer. The product worked, retention was solid, growth was healthy. The price was wrong, and by the time they noticed, 40,000 contracts were in the way of fixing it.

Pricing set before scale seems like a small decision. It's the biggest one you'll make, because growth doesn't correct bad pricing, it multiplies it.

## Growth locks in your mistakes

Every customer you acquire at the wrong price becomes a customer you have to re-sell, grandfather, or anger. Announcing a 3x increase to a base that tolerated your product at $29 is a churn event, not a pricing update.

There's also a deeper trap. Your price shapes who buys, which shapes what feedback you get, which shapes what you build. Sell too cheap and you attract buyers who care about cost more than outcomes. Their feature requests pull your roadmap toward busywork, and two years later you've built a product for the wrong market.

Wrong pricing compounds in three directions at once: revenue per customer stays low, customer quality degrades, and the roadmap bends around it.

## Founders price too low for three reasons

The first is fear. A number that feels uncomfortable in your head usually feels fine to the market, and the only way to know is to try it.

The second is confusion between value and effort. You price based on what the product costs you to build, not what it's worth to the customer. A tool that saves a logistics company $80,000 a quarter isn't worth $29 a month because it took you a weekend to write.

The third is premature comparison. Looking at competitors and undercutting by 20% assumes their pricing is informed. Often it's just an old number nobody revisited.

## Price first, then find scale

Do the pricing work before you pour money into acquisition:

- Interview five buyers about what the outcome is worth to them, in dollars.
- Test two or three price points with real checkout pages, not surveys.
- Pick the highest price that doesn't kill conversion, then build growth on top of it.

Volume at a healthy margin beats volume at a thin one, every time. It funds the team, it funds the support, and it gives you room to discount strategically instead of reflexively.

Put a real price in front of real buyers this week, before you spend another dollar on acquisition.
