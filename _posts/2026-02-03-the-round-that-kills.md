A startup sells for $120 million. The founders celebrate for about a week, then their lawyer sends the waterfall model. After the preferences are paid, they walk away with $1.8 million split four ways. The investors who wrote checks in Series A and B take nearly everything.

This happens more often than the pitch decks suggest. The exit was fine. The terms were not.

## Liquidation preferences are the whole story

A liquidation preference says investors get paid before common shareholders when the company is sold. A 1x preference is standard: put in a dollar, get a dollar back first. Trouble starts when preferences stack.

Raise $30 million across three rounds with 1x preferences, and the first $30 million of any exit goes to investors. That part is normal. The damage comes from participating preferred, where investors take their money back and then also share the remaining proceeds like ordinary stockholders. A 2x preference doubles the problem by requiring twice the investment back before anyone else sees cash.

Read your term sheet for these three things: the multiple, whether the preference participates, and whether it caps. A participating preference without a cap is the clause that empties founder pockets in mid-sized exits.

## Success is what makes it lethal

Nobody notices these terms when the company is worth $5 million or $500 million. The first is too small to sell, the second is large enough that preferences are a rounding error.

The kill zone is the $40 million to $200 million exit. Enough money for preferences to absorb nearly all of it, not enough for the leftovers to matter. Founders who raised heavy rounds at low valuations are the most exposed, because preferences are fixed dollar amounts while their equity percentage was already diluted.

Board members rarely flag this during fundraising. They're on both sides of the table.

## What to actually negotiate

You can't skip preferences entirely, investors won't fund without them. You can control the shape:

- Push for non-participating preferred. It's the market standard in most early rounds.
- Cap participation at 2x or 3x if someone insists on participating.
- Ask for a multiple above 1x only in later, riskier rounds where it's genuinely justified.
- Model the waterfall at $50M, $100M, and $250M exits before you sign anything.

One spreadsheet, thirty minutes, before the money moves. Founders who run those numbers negotiate differently, founders who don't find out at closing.

Run the waterfall model on your own term sheet this week, before the next round.
